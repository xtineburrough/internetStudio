Assignment 2 Guide

Press each button to reveal the steps to completing the first project.

Begin by completing Chapters 3 and 4. After you create the "New York Felines" example you are ready to design your hyperlink narrative, complete with Bootstrap's responsive features. To get inspiration view Olia Lialina's My Boyfriend Came Back from the War.
Revisit Exercise 4.2.1 to create a new repo from our template. Basically, you are telling a non-linear story. So name your new repo "story." Add Bootstrap to the index page as you do in Exercise 4.2.1. You do not need to use buttons in your story if you do not want them (and if you do not want them, then don't complete steps 7 and 8).
Sketch your story on paper. Create a hierarchy structure, like a tree, a sequence, or series of nodes, to plan where your story pages will link. Not sure where to start? This article about website structure will help you!
When you are ready to code your project I strongly suggest naming your pages by number, in the order of your structure (ie. 02.html, 03.html, 04.html, and so on). I also suggest adding those numbers to your sketch! Edit your work in Codespaces and upload images you need to the appropriate folder. If you get stuck on file paths and uploading, revisit Exercise 3.2.3. You can use Google's View > Developer View to see here how I would create a link to go to page two using the a tag.
Stage and Commit your changes in Codespaces, then return to your GitHub.com repository. This is the big moment. Make sure you have enabled GitHub Pages from your Repository Settings. Take a look at your Deployments. The live version of your page should be at githubusername.io/story/index.html but if it is not, it will be at http://yourGithubUsername.io/theNameOfTheRepoWhereYouSavedYourWork/theFileNameOfYourHyperlinkNarrative.html

For instance, if your username is janie and you saved a file called "hyperlink.html" inside your on-the-grid repo, your live page would be found at https://janie.github.io/on-the-grid/hyperlink.hml
